# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Enterprise teams that run knowledge-heavy, review-heavy work and need to turn it into governed apps: regulated market monitoring, product and service operations, proposal response, field support, document operations, internal tooling, and expert review workflows.

## Product Purpose

The landing page must make Blinkin feel like a real app building and deployment platform connected to enterprise knowledge, not a generic AI assistant. It should show that the platform spans the full lifecycle of business work: connect trusted sources, define workflow logic, capture live signals, discuss and highlight evidence, evaluate items, build structured tables, generate documents, run workflows, create runnable apps, publish outputs, and keep governance visible.

## Platform Position

Blinkin combines the legacy builder model of Spaces, Agents, Studio Canvas, Apps or Blinks, and Companions with the newer platform surface shown in the product: Monitor, Knowledge Spaces, Notebooks, Chat, Signal Review, Editor, Table Review, Artifacts, Workflows, Evaluation, Data Controls, Published outputs, and External Users.

The core message is that business teams do not need another isolated chat box or a builder disconnected from company knowledge. They need one controlled place where enterprise knowledge, app logic, live inputs, expert judgment, generated outputs, deployment surfaces, and reusable memory can work together.

The closed-loop logic is central: connect trusted knowledge, configure the workflow and surface, capture live signals, process into output, review and approve, deliver and learn. Only expert-approved outcomes should become durable memory or reusable workflow logic.

## Brand Personality

Operational, exacting, confident, and product-led. The tone should be concrete and capability-rich. Name the real app or workflow people need, then show the knowledge, review, and deployment surfaces that make it credible.

## Anti-References

Avoid vague "AI productivity" claims, chatbot-first positioning, decorative AI imagery, generic feature grids, abstract workflow metaphors with no product surface, and slogans that sound like internal notes. Do not reduce the page to "customer problems"; the visitor is the customer, so the page should speak directly about the app and workflow outcomes they can build.

## Design Principles

1. Show the product breadth early: Knowledge Spaces, Connectors, Agents, Studio, Monitor, Editor, Table, Artifacts, Workflows, Evaluation, and Data Controls.
2. Use current platform screenshots as evidence, not decoration.
3. Make knowledge feel operational and deployable: sources, source sets, inboxes, files, memory targets, notebooks, highlights, approvals, companion apps, APIs, and runnable artifacts.
4. Show the dark Monitor surface and the light workspace/editor surfaces so the page feels like the actual platform.
5. Keep the page sharp and product-led, with concrete module names, deployed app surfaces, and business outcomes.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast, keyboard accessible navigation and tab controls, visible focus states, responsive layouts, useful image alt text, and reduced-motion support.
